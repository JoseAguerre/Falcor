Dear Editor-in-Chief,

I am submitting my manuscript, "LeafAlias: Real-Time Importance Sampling of Dynamic
Emissive Textures for Video-Based Lighting" (Submission ID: papers_353s1), for
consideration for publication in ACM Transactions on Graphics.

Emissive textures used as light sources are traditionally treated as static images, and
state-of-the-art importance samplers (dense 2D CDF inversion, mipmap hierarchies, per-pixel
alias tables) are built once and reused for an entire sequence. This assumption breaks down
in an increasingly common class of workflows -- LED-wall virtual production, AR/VR object
insertion, and video-based environment maps -- where the emissive texture itself changes
every frame and the sampling structure must be rebuilt in real time.

This paper introduces LeafAlias, a family of samplers that build a per-frame alias table
over a variance-driven quadtree partition rather than over raw pixels, in two variants:
CodecLeafAlias, which reuses the coding-tree partition that modern video codecs already
compute as part of standard video decoding, and BudgetLeafAlias, which builds a
budget-constrained quadtree from scratch when no codec metadata is available. Both are
inexpensive enough to rebuild every frame, small enough to remain resident in the GPU's L1
cache, and preserve more of the input sample set's spatial coherence than a per-pixel alias
table -- which we show produces a measurable periodic banding artifact in its sample
spectrum. At a matched real-time frame-time budget, our technique reduces RMSE by 9-53%
over dense 2D CDF inversion, depending on the scene, and reduces sampler memory and
per-frame transfer by several orders of magnitude relative to state-of-the-art samplers.

We believe this work is a good fit for TOG because it identifies and exploits a previously
unused connection between two mature toolchains -- video compression and real-time
rendering -- to solve a sampling problem that existing static-image techniques cannot
address efficiently, and validates it with a full real-time rendering pipeline across
several video-lit scenes rather than isolated sampling benchmarks.

This manuscript is original work, has not been published previously, and is not currently
under review at any other journal or conference. All authors have approved the manuscript
and agree with its submission.

Thank you for your consideration.
